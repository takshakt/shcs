## PAGE: about.html — About the Society

**Meta title:** About the Society | Sanatan Hindu Cultural Society, Sutton
**Meta description:** The story, vision, mission and objectives of the Sanatan Hindu Cultural Society, a registered charity serving the Hindu community of Sutton and neighbouring areas.

### Hero
**Heading:** About the Society
**Subheading:** A community group formed by and for people of Hindu beliefs in Sutton and the neighbouring areas.

### Section: Who we are
An estimated 8,000 Hindu people live in Sutton and the neighbouring areas. Until now, they have had no common place for worship, prayer, or cultural and religious functions. The Sanatan Hindu Cultural Society was formed to meet that need. We are a UK Registered Charity, number 1197115, established with the goal of building a Mandir and Community Centre in Sutton.

### Section: Our vision
To promote the principles and common understandings of the Sanatan Hindu Dharma among the members and society.

### Section: Our mission
- **A common platform.** To provide a shared place for people of Hindu beliefs in Sutton and the neighbouring areas to perform prayer, worship, and cultural and religious functions.
- **Religious guidance.** To provide guidance and facilities for religious functions, and for the religious rites connected with births, marriages and deaths.
- **Environmental awareness.** To raise awareness of global warming and encourage people towards the UK government's goal of net zero carbon emissions.
- **Community integration.** To help newly arriving Hindus in the area integrate with wider British society.

### Section: Our objectives
- To facilitate discussion of Hindu concepts across the various schools of Hindu philosophy, and the prime values of the Hindu way of life.
- To organise yoga, sports and meditation workshops for physical and mental wellbeing.
- To organise charitable events raising funds for natural calamities around the world, and to support local charities with a similar cause.
- To hold workshops and seminars on the environment and the effects of global warming.

### Section: Our community in action
Our work is practical as well as spiritual. During the pandemic we delivered food boxes to hospital staff, the homeless and the police. Our festivals at Manor Park and Rose Hill Park have drawn thousands of attendees, together with the Mayor of Sutton and council representatives. The Society's history is one of steady service to the borough.

### Section: History and the road to a Mandir
SHCS has been a registered charity for the past five years, formed with a single long-term aim: a permanent Mandir and Community Centre for Sutton. That aim is now within reach. We have won the auction for a religious property near Carshalton Beeches station, a building in continuous use for public worship since its construction in 1936. The remaining task is to fund its fit-out and establishment.

### Section: Our festival year
- **Maha Shivratri** (February/March). The great night of Lord Shiva, with prayers, fasting and abhishekam ceremonies.
- **Holi** (March). The festival of colours, marking the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil. Our annual Rang Barse event at Manor Park draws over 1,000 attendees.
- **Ram Navami** (March/April). The birth of Lord Rama, with special prayers, kirtans and prasad distribution.
- **Hanuman Jayanti** (March/April). Celebrating Lord Hanuman's birth with Hanuman Chalisa recitations and special prayers.
- **Janmashtami** (August). Lord Krishna's birthday, with fasting, prayers and festivities including dahi handi.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi** (August/September). The birthday of Lord Ganesha, with special pujas, modak offerings and community celebrations.
- **Navratri** (September/October). Nine nights of worship to Goddess Durga, with garba, dandiya and devotional celebrations.
- **Dussehra** (October). The victory of good over evil. Our Ravan Dahan event features a 25-foot effigy and a fireworks display.
- **Diwali** (October/November). The festival of lights, celebrating the return of Lord Rama, with prayers, diyas, fireworks and a community feast.

### Section: What to expect when you visit
Our events are open to everyone, of every background and faith. Families and children are welcome. Most festival gatherings include prayer, devotional music and prasad, and larger events add food stalls and cultural performances. Some events require registration or tickets in advance; details are given with each listing. If you are unsure about anything, contact us at info@suttonmandir.org.uk and we will be glad to help.

**Heading:** Rang Barse: Holi fills Manor Park with colour
On 24 March, SHCS held its annual Rang Barse Holi celebration at Manor Park. More than 1,000 people from Sutton and beyond attended. The day marked the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil, with DJ music, dhol drumming, and unlimited colours for everyone to play with.

### Article 2
**Date:** 24 October 2023
**Heading:** Ravan Dahan at Rose Hill Park draws 5,000 people
On the evening of 24 October, SHCS hosted its Ravan Dahan celebration at Rose Hill Park. Around 5,000 people attended. The centrepiece was the ceremonial burning of a 25-foot Ravan effigy, marking the victory of good over evil, followed by a professional fireworks display. Indian street food stalls served traditional dishes throughout the evening.

The Mayor of Sutton, Councillor Colin Stears, and Council Leader Ruth Dombey were guests of honour. Both spoke of the event's inclusive spirit and its role in promoting cultural understanding within the borough. The evening was made possible by SHCS volunteers and sponsors, whose work covered everything from crafting the effigy to running the programme.

### Section: The property and its history
The building stands near Carshalton Beeches station. Its use as a place of public worship and religious instruction is long established. Registration records shared by the Trustees of the Clifton Hall Gospel Hall Trust show the property was registered as a place of religious worship at Somerset House in June 1936, and as a building for the solemnisation of marriages in 1974. A Statement of Truth will be provided via solicitors at completion, confirming continuous use for public worship and religious instruction since its construction in 1936, together with the original registration documents.

### Section: The Mandir plan
The Mandir – Community Centre will be a Hindu Sanatan Mandir with no primary deity. With the grace of the Almighty, we expect to install the following deities to begin with: Ram Darbar, Shivalay, Hanuman ji, Ganapati, Radha Krishna, Vitthal Rukmini, Balaji, Sharda Mata / Saraswati, Durga Mata and Murugan.
